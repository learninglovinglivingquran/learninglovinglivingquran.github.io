Three Ways of Knowing Something Is True

Testimony

So many independent reports that a lie couldn't survive intact. Secure — but second-hand.

Reasoning

An honest argument, followed to its conclusion. Legitimate — but still an inference.

Direct Experience

Perceived yourself — the same directness you just imagined a moment ago.

Almost everything we take on faith can only be known the first two ways. The Qur'an claims something unusual: its own miracle can be examined directly, in the Arabic itself, by anyone equipped to look. Not a promise of a dramatic moment for everyone — a door that opens.

قَرَأَ
If this means "he read" — is that everything that's there?

The same root gives us Qurʾān — not "a book," but "a recitation," meant to be gathered and read aloud. "He read" is accurate. It's also a fraction of what's there.

Science & Art

Today: Science

Arabic has a precise, rule-based side (nahw/sarf) and a rhetorical side (balāghah) where rules bend on purpose. Today is the first kind, cold — you can't bend a rule you don't know.

The Tool

Ism, Fiʿl, Ḥarf

Every word in Arabic is exactly one of three things:

اسم
ism · noun

Names a person, thing, place, or quality.

فِعْل
fiʿl · verb

An action or state, with time built in.

حَرْف
ḥarf · connector

"In," "to," "and" — never stands alone.

Fastest test: does the word carry a sense of time? If yes, fiʿl. Ism never does.

خَلَقَ الْإِنسَانَ
"He created mankind"
خَلَقَfiʿl
الْإِنسَانَism
فِي بَيْتِهِ
"In his house"
فِيḥarf
بَيْتِهِism
ال attached to a word is always a noun-signal — no exceptions.
