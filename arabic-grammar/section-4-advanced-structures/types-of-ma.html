The many jobs of مَا in Arabic

One tiny word, two letters, and at least eight different jobs. Five of them you may already know. This post covers the other three: the extra مَا, the مَا that makes a verbal noun, and the مَا of time.

November 14, 20268 min read

You have met مَا (mā) many times. It can mean "what," "not," "whatever," and more. The trick in reading the Quran is to ask which job it is doing in the sentence in front of you, because the answer changes the meaning.

The five you may already know

Before the new ones, here is a quick refresher on the five uses covered earlier.

Type What it does Example
مَا الْمَوْصُولَة
relative		 "whatever" or "that which" سَبَّحَ لِلَّهِ مَا فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ
"Whatever is in the heavens and the earth glorifies Allah." The opening of Al-Hadid, Al-Hashr and As-Saff.
مَا الِاسْتِفْهَامِيَّة
question		 "what?" وَمَا رَبُّ الْعَالَمِينَ
"And what is the Lord of the worlds?" Ash-Shu'ara 26:23.
مَا النَّافِيَة
negating		 "not" وَمَا هُم بِخَارِجِينَ مِنَ النَّارِ
"They will not be leaving the Fire." Al-Baqarah 2:167.
مَا الشَّرْطِيَّة
conditional		 "whatever ... then" وَمَا تُنفِقُوا مِن شَيْءٍ فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ يُوَفَّ إِلَيْكُمْ
"Whatever you spend in Allah's way will be repaid to you in full." Al-Anfal 8:60.
مَا التَّعَجُّبِيَّة
amazement		 "how ...!" فَمَا أَصْبَرَهُمْ عَلَى النَّارِ
"How stubbornly they cling to the Fire!" Al-Baqarah 2:175.

Now for the remaining three.

1. The extra مَا (مَا الزَّائِدَة)

زَائِدَة means "extra." That does not mean the word is useless. It means the sentence would still be complete and correct without it. What it adds is emphasis.

You have seen this idea before. The extra مِنْ gives the sense of "not a single," and the extra بِ gives the sense of "not at all." The extra مَا works the same way: it turns up the volume on the sentence it sits in.

Inside a phrase

Normally a preposition sits right next to its noun, and a described word sits right next to its description. The extra مَا can step in between them. Grammarians call it غَيْر كَافَّة, which here simply means it does not block anything: the preposition still works on its noun as before, and the sentence stays tied together.

Here مَا sits between the preposition بِ and its noun رَحْمَةٍ. The "only" in the translation comes from the phrase being placed before the verb, which narrows the focus to that one cause. The extra مَا adds to the force.

This time مَا sits between a described noun, صُورَةٍ ("form"), and its description, the verb phrase شَاءَ. Again the phrase is placed before the verb.

Inside a sentence

In a full sentence the extra مَا can show up in either a verbal or a nominal sentence. It has no fixed position, as long as it is somewhere inside the body of the sentence. Sentences that use it also tend to rearrange their parts, so expect the normal word order to be shuffled.

The most common form in the Quran is قَلِيلًا مَّا تَفْعَلُونَ, "how little you do." The object comes first, then مَا, then the verb.

It also appears in nominal sentences. In the next example, مَا sits between the predicate قَلِيلٌ and the subject هُمْ, and the predicate has been moved to the front.

In conditional sentences

The extra مَا also appears in conditionals, usually after إِنْ. It is written either as two words, إِنْ مَا, or joined as إِمَّا. In the Quran it always comes together with the نُون التَّوْكِيد (the emphatic noon) on the verb.

What does it emphasize? The condition itself. It makes the "if" much more likely to happen, something like "if this happens, and it surely will."

This is addressed to Maryam after the birth of 'Īsā. She was very likely to meet someone, so the extra مَا is used.

The ayah is about parents growing old, which is likely to happen if they live long enough. Notice that it still uses إِنْ and not إِذَا, because it is not certain that everyone reaches old age.

Do not confuse it with the emphatic lām The emphatic لَام also shows up on لَئِنْ, but it does a different job. The extra مَا means "if this happens, and it surely will," and it emphasizes the condition. The emphatic لَام means "I swear, if this happens," and it emphasizes the result.

The extra مَا can also go with إِذَا. Since إِذَا already implies that something will happen, مَا cannot make it more likely. Instead it adds a feeling of "finally" or "actually."

2. The مَا that makes a verbal noun (مَا الْمَصْدَرِيَّة)

In earlier studies you met two tools that turn a verb into a "fake" verbal noun, called a مَصْدَر مُؤَوَّل. They are أَنْ and أَنَّ. مَا is a third tool that does the same job.

A good test: replace مَا and the clause after it with the actual verbal noun. If the sentence still makes sense, it is this kind of مَا.

You could swap بِمَا أَوْحَيْنَا إِلَيْكَ for بِإِيحَائِنَا إِلَيْكَ and keep the same meaning. Notice that "we" stays in the translation ("our revealing"), and the verb becomes an "-ing" word.

Here مِمَّا تُجْرِمُونَ could be replaced with مِنْ إِجْرَامِكُمْ, "of your wrongdoing." The pronoun "you" stays in the translation, and the noun "wrongdoing" appears.

Verbal-noun مَا or relative مَا?

Some ayat can be read either way. This can only happen when there is no عَائِد, the pronoun that returns to the relative مَا. The verbal-noun مَا never has one. When the pronoun is missing, the reading you choose changes the translation.

In the verbal-noun reading, the idea of an action stays in the translation, and "they" is kept as "their." It is like saying سَاءَ عَمَلُهُمْ.

3. The مَا of time (مَا الزَّمَانِيَّة)

This مَا means "as long as" or "as much as." The verb after it is always translated in the present tense, even when the verb itself is in the past tense.

The verb اسْتَطَعْتُمْ is past tense, but we say "you are able."

The three at a glance

Type Meaning or effect How to spot it
مَا الزَّائِدَة
extra		 Emphasis. Strengthens a phrase, a sentence or a condition. Take it out and the sentence still works. Often has a lot of rearranged word order.
مَا الْمَصْدَرِيَّة
verbal noun		 Turns the clause into an "-ing" idea. Replace it and its clause with the verbal noun. No returning pronoun.
مَا الزَّمَانِيَّة
time		 "As long as" or "as much as." Followed by a verb that is translated in the present tense.

Why it matters

The same two letters can be a question, a denial, a condition, an exclamation, a relative, an intensifier, a verbal noun or a measure of time. When you read an ayah, ask what مَا is doing before you translate it. Often the clue is in the surrounding words: a returning pronoun, a following noon of emphasis, a verb that behaves oddly in tense.

And when مَا seems unnecessary, it may be the extra one, quietly turning up the emphasis.

Key takeaway Three more jobs for مَا: the extra one adds emphasis, the verbal-noun one turns a clause into an "-ing" idea, and the time one means "as long as." Some ayat can be read two ways, and the reading changes the translation.
