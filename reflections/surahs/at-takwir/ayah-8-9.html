Reflections

When the baby girl is asked

Among the most staggering events of the Day of Judgment, one question is singled out: for what sin was she killed?

At-Takwir 81:8–9November 14, 20263 min read

These are personal reflections on two ayat, offered as personal understanding and not as tafsir. Please check them against the scholars.

Next to the mountains and the stars

The surah opens with events most of us would call monumental: the sun rolled up, the stars scattered, the mountains set in motion. In the middle of that list, Allah mentions a baby girl and the injustice done to her.

It is worth sitting with that. We would never put the killing of a child on the same level as the stars falling. Many people, even after terrible wrongs, would not treat it as a big deal. Yet in the ayah it stands in the same breath as the end of the world.

Justice without conditions

The ayah sets no conditions. It does not say she was a Muslim, or that the one who buried her was not. There is no other implication. Something unfair was done to a child, and that is enough for it to be raised on that Day.

Before the Day has even properly begun

In the picture the ayah paints, this is among the first questions raised, almost as though Allah is demanding the answer before the Day has properly started. Justice for the wronged does not wait.

It also gives a different picture of the Day of Judgment. We can think of it mostly as Muslims winning and non-Muslims losing. This ayah shows it first as pure justice: someone wronged, and an answer demanded.

The one who is asked

Notice who is asked. The question is put to her, not to the one who did it. A girl who had no voice in this world, who was buried where no one could object, is the one addressed on that Day. Whatever else the ayah teaches, it shows that the voiceless are not forgotten and the unheard are given a hearing. This can be a comfort to anyone who has been wronged and was never listened to.

Questions to reflect on

Do I treat harm done to the weak and the small as seriously as Allah does?
Do I care about justice only when it involves "my side"?
Key takeaway Allah places the wrong done to one helpless child alongside the greatest events of the Day of Judgment. Justice there has no conditions attached, and the one who had no voice is the one addressed.
