Al-Fatiha 1–4: who are we speaking to?

Companion page for session 1 of the Quran in prayer circle. Four verses, five key words, and one question to carry into your next prayer.

November 11, 20265 min read

You recite Al-Fatiha at least seventeen times a day. This page slows down on its first four verses, which are about who you are speaking to before you ask for anything. Read the verses, look at the key words, then take the reflection question into your next prayer.

The verses

Key words

The roots below are the three-letter families these words come from. Words that share a root share a core idea, which is why the root is worth knowing.

Arabic Root Common translation Deeper meaning
الْحَمْدُal-ḥamd ح م د praise Often set apart from shukr (thanks). Praise is for who Allah is, not only for what He gives.
رَبّrabb ر ب ب Lord The one who nurtures and sustains something as it grows, step by step.
الرَّحْمَٰنar-raḥmān ر ح م The Most Merciful Shares a root with the word for womb: a protective, enveloping mercy.
الرَّحِيمar-raḥīm ر ح م The Especially Merciful Same root as ar-raḥmān. Commentators discuss how the two names relate (see Context).
مَالِكmāliki م ل ك Master, Owner The one who holds ownership and authority. Here, over the Day of Recompense.
الدِّينad-dīn د ي ن Recompense The day when everything is accounted for. The same root is also used for obedience and a way of life.

Key insight

Carry this into prayer Before Al-Fatiha asks for anything, it spends four verses on who you are speaking to: the One who sustains you, whose mercy surrounds you, and who is Master of the Day of Recompense.

Context

The Prophet ﷺ reported that Allah said He has divided the prayer between Himself and His servant. When the servant says الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ, Allah responds that His servant has praised Him. The next two verses are answered in turn. This is narrated in Sahih Muslim, and it is why these verses are a conversation, not only a recitation.

Ibn Kathir's tafsir discusses the two names ar-raḥmān and ar-raḥīm, both from the same root, and how scholars have understood the difference between them.

Reflect

Where did you feel nurtured or sustained this week, even in something difficult?

There is no right answer. Think of one moment, however small, and hold on to it.

This week's practice

When you recite rabbi l-ʿālamīn in your next prayer, pause for a moment on the word رَبّ, and bring to mind the moment you thought of above.
