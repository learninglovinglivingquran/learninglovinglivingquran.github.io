# LQ — Master Plan
### The systematic plan for building, maintaining, and growing the LQ resource network
*v1.1 — September 2026*

---

## Why this document exists

LQ has grown fast and changed shape more than once — the audience framing changed, the newsletter was retired, the blog was rebuilt, categories were renamed, the identity-recovery layer was added. Each change was good, but none of it happened inside a system, so keeping track of what's decided, what's in flight, and what's next has depended on memory and on re-reading old docs. This document is the fix: a single, systematic breakdown of everything LQ needs, organized into workstreams, each with a clear purpose, current state, open decisions, and next actions — plus a tracking system (below) so this stays current instead of going stale like the old website_structure.md and content_calendar.md did.

This document is the **strategic layer** — it changes slowly (review quarterly, or whenever a workstream's direction genuinely shifts). Day-to-day status lives in two companion docs, described in Workstream 0 below:
- **LQ Workstream Tracker** — current status of every workstream, updated weekly or biweekly
- **LQ Decision Log** — an append-only record of every real decision made, with rationale, so nothing gets re-litigated or forgotten

---

## The Vision (recap, for anchoring)

LQ exists because the modern person is harder to reach spiritually than past generations were — not because truth changed, but because the material world is more manifest and more bewitching now than it was for people who developed the classical approaches to the Quran. Those approaches assume a baseline spiritual openness that fewer people today actually have. LQ's path inverts the order: it starts where the modern person actually stands — reason, logic, material and psychological benefit — and uses that ground to walk toward the Quran as the destination, rather than assuming the destination as the starting point.

Everything below serves that one inversion. If a workstream doesn't clearly serve the "meet them in reason, lead them to the Quran" pathway, it doesn't belong on the list yet.

## Workstream 0 — Planning & Tracking Infrastructure

**Purpose:** Make it structurally impossible for LQ to feel haphazard again. This is the operating system the other nine workstreams run on.

**Components:**

1. **This document (LQ Master Plan)** — the strategic reference. Answers "what is LQ trying to do and why." Reviewed quarterly.
2. **LQ Workstream Tracker** (companion doc) — answers "where does each workstream stand right now." A living status table: workstream, status (Not Started / Planning / Active / Blocked / Paused / Done-for-now), last updated, current next action, blocked-on (if any). Updated in a short weekly or biweekly pass — not after every small edit, but often enough that it's never more than ~2 weeks stale.
3. **LQ Decision Log** (companion doc) — answers "why is it this way." Append-only. Every time a real decision gets made (a name, a platform, a structural change, a scope cut), it gets one dated line: what was decided, what the alternative(s) were, why this one won. This is what stops the "wait, why did we rename that category again?" problem, and it's what future-you (or a scholar/collaborator you eventually bring in) reads to get oriented fast.
4. **Capacity budget** (see Workstream 9) — an honest, standing answer to "how much LQ time actually exists per week," checked before adding anything new to the tracker.

**Cadence discipline:** Three review rhythms, not one:
- *Weekly/biweekly* — 10–15 minutes, update the Tracker, log any decisions made.
- *Monthly* — review the content calendar (Workstream 3) against what actually got published; adjust the schedule honestly rather than silently letting it drift.
- *Quarterly* — revisit this Master Plan itself. Is the phasing (below) still right? Has the vision sharpened again? Retire or add workstreams as needed.

**Open decisions:** none.

## Workstream 1 — Content Standards (Templates, Voice, Quality Control)

**Purpose:** Make sure a piece of content is recognizably LQ no matter which category, medium, or day it was made on — and make sure nothing goes out that's factually or theologically shaky.

**Covers your item 5 (templates), plus the QC/review gap identified in review.**

**Components:**

1. **Post template per category.** Each of the five blog categories (Legible World, An Arabic Quran, Arabic Dictionary, Topical Quran Reflections, Quran Reflection) gets a structural template: what sections a piece in that category always has, in what order, at what rough length, with what kind of opening and closing. This is distinct from the visual design system (already documented in LQ_Theme_Reference.md) — this is the *content* skeleton, not the CSS.
2. **A living style/voice guide.** The tone section already exists in the Overview doc (calm, intelligent, personal, non-preachy) and so does the core vocabulary (the thread, the exile, the amnesiac, the rupture, huda, ummah-as-quality). This needs to become an actual checklist content gets checked against before publishing — not just a paragraph someone might remember to reread.
3. **A QC / accuracy review pass**, required before anything publishes. LQ makes specific, checkable claims: historical (the Babylon/Ibrahim, Pharaoh, Haman archaeology), scientific/philosophical (fine-tuning, the five rational questions), and theological (huda, ummah, tafsir-adjacent readings of specific ayat). Each needs at minimum a self-check against primary sources before publishing, and — given how central these claims are to LQ's credibility — you should decide whether some categories (especially Quran Reflection and Topical Reflections, which are closest to tafsir) warrant a second reader with more formal training. This is a real open decision, not just a to-do.

**Open decisions:**
- Do any content categories require a scholarly second-reader, or is rigorous self-review (cited sources, cross-checked claims) sufficient for all of them?
- Where does the style/voice checklist live and who checks it — self-check before publish, or a separate pass?

**Next actions:**
- [ ] Draft the per-category post templates (5)
- [ ] Convert the existing tone/vocabulary sections into a checklist format
- [ ] Decide the QC review model (see open decisions) and log it in the Decision Log once settled

A common trap is spreading too thin across all five categories at once — decide whether to go deep on one or two categories first (Legible World and An Arabic Quran already have momentum) before seeding the other three.

## Workstream 4 — Content Architecture & Cross-Linking

**Purpose:** Make LQ actually function as *one network*, not five parallel resources someone has to already know about to find.

**Identified as a gap in the review — not in your original list, but essential given LQ is explicitly five interconnected resources.**

**Components:**

1. **The user journey map.** For each entry point (a YouTube short, a Reddit comment, a Twitter/X post, a Google search landing on a blog post), what's the intended next step? The site's CTAs previously pointed at a placeholder; now that lqpathway.com and all four social handles are real, this can actually be built.
2. **Internal cross-linking rules.** Which blog posts should link to which other posts (e.g., a Legible World piece linking to the relevant Arabic Toolkit session when it touches the linguistic-miracle argument)? Which YouTube videos should describe-link back to which blog posts? Does the app eventually pull from blog content, or is it a separate track?
3. **The "door" journey, made concrete.** Door One and Door Two are conceptually distinct entry points but need an actual routing mechanism — does the site or the content itself help a reader self-identify which door they came in through, or is that left implicit in the writing? (Door Two's routing is lower priority while Essay Two is shelved.)

**Open decisions:**
- Does content explicitly route Door One vs. Door Two readers, or stay implicit?
- Reddit's specific role in the journey — it behaves more like a discussion/outreach venue than a broadcast channel the way YouTube/X/Instagram do; worth a distinct usage plan rather than treating all four platforms identically.

**Next actions:**
- [ ] Sketch the user journey map for the current live resources (website + the four social profiles once active)
- [ ] Add a lightweight cross-linking pass to the post template (Workstream 1) so it's built in from the start, not retrofitted later
- [ ] Draft a short Reddit-specific usage note (engagement style differs from the other three)

## Workstream 6 — Social Media & Outreach

**Purpose:** Get LQ in front of people, both through ongoing general marketing and through per-piece amplification.

**Covers your items 6 and 8.**

**Current state:** Handles claimed on all four platforms — @LQPathway (YouTube, X), lq.pathway (Instagram), u/LQPathway (Reddit). Claiming a handle isn't the same as an active, populated profile — that setup work is still ahead.

**Components:**

1. **Profile setup.** For each platform: bio, profile image/branding consistent with the LQ design system, link to lqpathway.com, and (where relevant) an initial post so the profile doesn't look abandoned when someone clicks through from the website.
2. **Overall marketing strategy** (item 8) — voice and cadence for general-audience posts: what does LQ post about that isn't tied to a specific piece of content (threads on the rational questions, short standalone reflections, engaging with relevant conversations)? Reddit likely needs its own approach here — it rewards genuine participation in existing discussions over broadcast posting, unlike YouTube/X/Instagram.
3. **Per-piece social plan** (item 6) — for each blog post/essay/video, what social content gets made *from* it (a pull-quote graphic, a thread summarizing the argument, a short teaser). This should be built into the post template (Workstream 1) as a checklist item, not planned separately after the fact.
4. **Response/engagement plan.** With no newsletter and no email list, social media is currently LQ's only feedback channel. Worth deciding now whether there's any structured way to receive and respond to questions/pushback (comments, DMs, a contact form) — leaving this fully ad hoc is exactly the kind of thing that becomes unsustainable once there's any real audience.
5. **Wire the sitewide CTA.** Replace "Follow LQ on Social Media" (currently a `#` placeholder) with real links to the four profiles, once they're live enough to be worth sending someone to.

**Open decisions:**

## Workstream 9 — Capacity, Resourcing & Success Metrics

**Purpose:** Keep the plan honest. A plan with ten workstreams and no capacity check is exactly how things get haphazard again.

**Identified as a gap in review.**

**Components:**

1. **Capacity budget.** LQ runs alongside a full-time Trusted Advisor role and the Synqmatic side business, plus the Al-Safar/Tadabbur work now folding into it. An honest weekly time budget for LQ (even a rough number) should exist and be checked whenever the Tracker shows too many workstreams "Active" at once. Whoever else is involved (if anyone beyond you) should be reflected here too.
2. **Prioritization rule.** When capacity is limited, what wins — finishing a workstream in progress, or starting the highest-impact new one? A simple standing rule (e.g., "no more than 2 workstreams Active at once outside Workstream 0") prevents the ten-workstreams-all-half-done problem. (As of this update, Workstreams 5, 6, and 9 are all Active alongside 0 — worth consciously bringing one to Done-for-now before starting anything new.)
3. **Success metrics**, defined per resource, kept modest and honest:
   - Website: is it easy to find things, does it look intentional, is search bringing anyone in (not a vanity traffic target)
   - Content: is the reframing audit (Workstream 2) actually done, is the calendar (Workstream 3) being hit within a reasonable margin
   - Social: engagement quality over follower count, per platform (Reddit especially shouldn't be judged by follower-style metrics)
   - YouTube/App: these are later-phase, so metrics can wait until they're actually live
   
   The point of metrics here isn't growth-hacking — it's just having *some* answer to "is this working" beyond a feeling.

**Open decisions:** The actual weekly time budget number; the prioritization rule's exact shape.

**Next actions:**
- [ ] Set an honest weekly LQ time budget
- [ ] Adopt a simple prioritization rule and log it
- [ ] Define the modest success metrics above per currently-active resource (website + content + social, now that social is live)

## Phasing — how the ten workstreams actually get sequenced

Ten workstreams running at once is the haphazard pattern repeating itself. Given limited capacity (Workstream 9) and that content and infrastructure are furthest along, a natural phase order:

**Phase 1 — Foundation (now):**
- Workstream 0 (tracking/decision infrastructure) — ✅ stood up
- Workstream 9 (capacity budget + prioritization rule) — settle immediately, it governs everything else
- Workstream 5 (website infrastructure) — ✅ domain decided and live; Jekyll migration and SEO remain
- Workstream 2 (existing content remediation) — fix what's live before building more on top of it

**Phase 2 — Content Engine:**
- Workstream 1 (content standards: templates, voice, QC)
- Workstream 3 (content strategy & calendar)
- Workstream 4 (content architecture & cross-linking)
- Workstream 6 (social media & outreach) — ✅ platforms/handles decided; profile setup and CTA wiring remain

**Phase 3 — Expansion:**
- Workstream 7 (YouTube channel) — once there's a stable content base to draw from and reframe
- Workstream 8 (LQ app) — the slowest-build, most technically demanding resource; deliberately last

This isn't rigid — Workstream 0 and 9 should genuinely be finished this week, but Phases 2 and 3 will overlap in practice. The point is: nothing in Phase 3 should be "Active" in the Tracker while Phase 1 items are still unresolved.
